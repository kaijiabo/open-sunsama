**Pick Super Productivity if you work alone, track your time and want no server at all. Pick Vikunja if you want a task server you run yourself and share with family or a team.** That is the Super Productivity vs Vikunja choice in one line: an app on your device, or a server you own.

Both are open source and active: in November 2026 Super Productivity shipped v20.1.0 and Vikunja shipped v2.8.0. They just solve different problems.

Our pick for most people is a third option: [Open Sunsama](/alternative/sunsama). It plans your day on your real calendar and works with any AI agent. We build Open Sunsama, so we put it first — here is why, and where others fit better.

## Quick verdict

- **Pick Super Productivity if** you want timeboxing, time sheets and focus timers on one device, with no account.
- **Pick Vikunja if** you want a self-hosted Todoist, with kanban, Gantt and shared projects.
- **Pick Open Sunsama if** you want to plan your day on a calendar, let an AI agent help, and own your data. It is our pick for most people.

## How we compared them

In November 2026 we read each project's README, docs, license, release notes and feature pages. We also read r/selfhosted threads and GitHub issues where users compare the two.

We did not run a long trial of each app. We build and use Open Sunsama every day, and every fact about the other two links to its source.

## Where do your tasks live in each app?

**Super Productivity lives on your device.** You install it and start, with no sign-up and no server. Its site says it collects no data.

If you want your tasks on a second device, you turn on sync. You can use SuperSync, which is end-to-end encrypted and can be self-hosted, or Dropbox, or WebDAV.

**Vikunja lives on a server.** You run it with Docker or a single binary, then open it in a browser. Every device talks to that server.

That makes sharing easy. You can share a project with a person or a team, or send a link that needs no account. It also means you have one more service to keep running.

## Where does Super Productivity win?

### It tracks your time for you

Super Productivity was built for people who bill hours or want to see where the day went. It tracks time per task and turns it into time sheets and work summaries.

### It helps you focus

Its focus mode offers Pomodoro, Flowtime and countdown sessions. It also has a break reminder and an anti-procrastination helper.

### It pulls in work from dev tools

It can import tasks assigned to you from Jira, GitHub, GitLab, Gitea, Linear, ClickUp, Trello, OpenProject, Redmine, Nextcloud Deck and Azure DevOps. Developers like that their issues land in one list.

### It runs everywhere, with no account

There are apps for Linux, Mac, Windows, Android and iOS, plus a web app. The MIT license is as open as it gets.

Since v19.3.0 in October 2026, it also has an opt-in local MCP endpoint. Claude or ChatGPT can add tasks with schedules and repeats.

## Where does Vikunja win?

### It is a real task server

Vikunja is a self-hosted Todoist. You get lists, labels, priorities, due dates, reminders, recurring tasks and subtasks. You can also link tasks across projects.

### It has four views of the same tasks

Each project can show as a list, a kanban board, a Gantt chart or a table. Saved filters pull tasks from many projects into one view.

### It is built for sharing

You can share projects with users or teams and assign tasks. Families and small teams use it as a shared to-do list on their own server.

### It speaks open standards

Vikunja has a REST API, webhooks and CalDAV, so other apps can read your tasks. It imports from Todoist, Trello and Microsoft To Do.

In v2.7.0, released in October 2026, it added a built-in MCP server. Your AI assistant can now work with your Vikunja tasks.

If you don't want to run a server, [Vikunja Cloud](https://vikunja.io/) hosts it for you. The Personal plan costs €4 a month (November 2026).

## Where does each one fall short?

**Super Productivity** is built for one person. Sharing needs Plainspace, a separate service you connect. Its CalDAV support is basic, and its MCP endpoint runs only on your own device.

**Vikunja** has no day planner and no calendar to block time on. Time tracking sits in the paid Pro add-on, which is in private beta. Phones get a web app or an Android app in alpha, with no official iOS app.
